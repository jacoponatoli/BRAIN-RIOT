BRAIN RIOT
Jacopo Natoli, 2026

Riots of the whole world and of every time: clashes with the police, barricades, tear gas, fires. Every three hours a crawler walks further into Wikimedia Commons (guided by Wikidata), Openverse, Europeana and the Library of Congress, and the corpus keeps growing: … photographs so far. Every image belongs to its author and keeps its own license.

About one image in seven is invented: riots of robots, cyborgs, androids and the like, in the streets of the real riots. The crawler writes the scenes and a machine imagines them (Pollinations.AI), thirty or so every three hours: … so far. In the list below they are marked “invented”.

About one in five is a hybrid: real photographs and old engravings into which the page slips, each time differently, figures of robots and cyborgs that the machine imagines alone on white, photographed or engraved (… figures so far). No hybrid passes twice the same. Only pictures whose licence allows changes are altered; in the list below they are marked “altered”.

Now and then, rarely, words, big, over the images: classic slogans of the riots of every time, and the words of the protests of these days, picked up every three hours from the news and from Wikipedia (… so far). Calls to kill and words against a people, a faith or a community are left out. In the list below they are marked “a classic” or “words of” the day they were heard.
